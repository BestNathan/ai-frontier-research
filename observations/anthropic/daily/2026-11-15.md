# Evidence-Bound Review, Dynamic Agent Harnesses, and Scientific Model Search

**Observed:** 2026-11-15  
**Publication coverage:** 2026-06-02 to 2026-11-06 (three high-value technical backfills; no newly dated November 14–15 first-party engineering article verified in the reviewed listings)  
**Sources checked:** [Claude Resources](https://claude.com/resources), [Articles](https://claude.com/resources/articles), [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering), [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research), [Claude Developer Blog](https://claude.dev/).  
**Evidence status:** First-party reports and guest research perspective. Vendor measurements are not independent replications; no pinned implementation audit was performed.

## Engineering question and invariants

How should agent-generated work be orchestrated, independently checked and promoted into trustworthy outputs when both production software and scientific models can be generated faster than humans can inspect them?

- **Evidence attribution:** A review result must identify the exact code revision, requirements, checker and environment; an agent assertion is not a verified outcome.
- **Bounded orchestration:** Fan-out and repeated review must have finite resource budgets, failure propagation and deterministic stopping rules.
- **Separation of authority:** Untrusted research or code inputs should be processed with read-only capabilities before any privileged executor receives a validated, structured request.
- **Scientific identifiability:** Fitting observations does not uniquely identify a model or justify a mechanistic explanation.
- **Human accountability:** Risk and irreversible side effects determine escalation; automated agreement is not a substitute for independent evidence.

## 1. Code review as a risk-weighted, revision-bound verification pipeline

- **Original:** [How the Claude Code team does code review](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/)
- **Published:** 2026-10-15; **observed:** 2026-11-15
- **Classification:** First-party engineering disclosure, not an independently audited quality benchmark.
- **Domains:** [Verification](../../../domains/verification.md), [Delivery](../../../domains/delivery.md), [Security](../../../domains/security.md), [Evaluation](../../../domains/evaluation.md)

**Engineering problem:** When AI authors large numbers of PRs, line-by-line human reading becomes the scarce resource. Removing human review without replacing its error-detection and accountability functions can merely transfer time from review to incident response.

**Verified (publisher-reported):**

- The Claude Code team reports that between June and September its merged PR volume **more than doubled** while the reverted share **fell more than 40%**. These are observational figures with undisclosed confounders, not a controlled causal estimate.
- The pipeline begins with runnable verification and fresh-context subagent checks before PR creation; every PR receives AI bug review, a separate security review and blocking CI. Findings are verified by additional agents, severity-labelled, and require resolution or documented dismissal.
- Risk tiers determine required human approvals: low-risk may need none after automated gates; medium-risk one; high-risk two. Sensitive paths (including CI workflows) always require a human. A bot does not count as a required human approver. Approval is bound to a specific commit and may be invalidated by new changes.
- The merge queue tests the change against the current main integration state; staged flags, telemetry and rollback cover post-merge failures.
- Reviewer quality is evaluated against historical incident/revert PRs and known-clean PRs, including noise and harmful false positives. A review-fix loop exceeded **50 rounds** on one PR; the team adopted a **three-round reconsideration threshold**, incremental re-review and deduplication of findings. It reports roughly **59%** of repeatedly flagged code predated the first review pass.

**Inferred:** Model review should be treated as a fallible evidence-producing service, not an approval principal. Store `{head_sha, base_sha, policy_version, reviewer_version, finding_id, severity, disposition, test_run_id}` per review. Invalidate or narrow evidence after code changes. The true optimization target is human time per **correct** merged change, including post-merge intervention and rollback, not reviewer comments or PR throughput alone.

**Alternatives:** Mandatory human review for all changes (expensive, simple policy); deterministic checks only (low false positives but weak semantic coverage); agent review plus human risk tiers (broader coverage, calibration burden); automated low-risk merge with release gates and fast rollback (higher operational risk).

**Unknown:** Precise sample sizes, incident denominators, causal contribution of each layer, classifier error distribution, private tooling implementation and branch-protection configuration.

**Hypothesis / experiment:** Re-run a pinned historical PR corpus with labelled regressions, including clean controls and malicious changes. Compare deterministic-only, single-agent review, independent multi-agent review and human-assisted risk-tier review. Measure bug recall, precision, harmful suggested fixes, p50/p95 review latency, human minutes, rounds-to-convergence, reverts and incidents per 100 merged PRs. Inject a new commit after approval and confirm that evidence is invalidated; simulate a concurrent main advancement and verify merge-queue retesting. Pre-register confidence intervals and cost budget.

## 2. Dynamic task-specific harnesses trade orchestration cost for context isolation

- **Original:** [A harness for every task: dynamic workflows in Claude Code](https://claude.dev/blog/a-harness-for-every-task-dynamic-workflows-in-claude-code/)
- **Published:** 2026-06-02; **observed:** 2026-11-15
- **Classification:** First-party architecture and usage guidance; not a comparative production benchmark.
- **Domains:** [Orchestration](../../../domains/orchestration.md), [Durable execution](../../../domains/durable-execution.md), [Context and retrieval](../../../domains/context-and-retrieval.md), [Security](../../../domains/security.md), [Evaluation](../../../domains/evaluation.md)

**Engineering problem:** A single context can suffer goal drift, self-preferential verification and premature termination on long, adversarial or highly parallel tasks. A fixed multi-agent topology wastes compute when the problem does not require specialization.

**Verified (publisher-reported):**

- Claude Code dynamic workflows use a generated **JavaScript orchestration file** with `agent()`, `parallel()` and `pipeline()` composition. An agent call may specify structured schema, model and worktree/remote isolation. The post says an interrupted workflow can resume with its session.
- Documented patterns include classify-and-act, fan-out/synthesis, independent adversarial verification, generate/filter, tournament and loop-until-done. The article describes a quarantine pattern: read untrusted tickets in a low-privilege zone and pass structured findings to a privileged actor.
- Workflow scripts can be saved and distributed through skills; explicit token budgets are supported. The author cautions that workflows often consume significantly more tokens and are not warranted for ordinary coding tasks.
- These are disclosed product behaviors and examples; no precise guarantees are supplied for crash consistency, concurrent writes, deterministic replay, tool-effect idempotency or durable workflow history.

**Inferred:** Treat generated workflows as *plans requiring validation* before execution. A robust harness runtime should type-check tool schemas and graph edges, bound fan-out and recursion, enforce per-node capability policy and attach each tool effect to a task identity. Worktree isolation prevents some file conflicts but not shared service or external side effects. Session resume is not evidence of exactly-once tool execution.

**Alternatives:** One agent with tools/skills (low coordination cost, context coupling); fixed DAG engine (auditable and predictable, less adaptive); generated dynamic DAG (adaptive but validation burden); leader/subagent hierarchy (strong specialization, handoff and latency cost).

**Unknown:** Scheduler fairness, retry semantics, persistence granularity, worktree cleanup, cancellation and cost accounting of generated workflows under production failures.

**Hypothesis / experiment:** Pin harness/tool versions and a task suite spanning simple fixes, flaky-test root cause, 100-way independent analysis and multi-stage migration. Compare single loop, static DAG and generated DAG. Inject agent death, partial parallel results, duplicate tool calls, invalid generated code, cross-worktree shared-service writes and cancellation. Measure completion correctness, side-effect duplicates, p95 latency, token/GPU cost, human interventions and context-loss failures. Set maximum agents, depth and spend before the run.

## 3. Scientific-model agents must preserve competing hypotheses and uncertainty

- **Original:** [Teaching AI to build scientific models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/teaching-ai-to-build-scientific-models)
- **Published:** 2026-11-06; **observed:** 2026-11-15
- **Classification:** Guest research perspective by Jakob Macke, Daniel Gedon and Stefan Wahl, hosted by Anthropic; **not** a claim that Anthropic deployed a particular scientific-modeling platform.
- **Domains:** [Evaluation](../../../domains/evaluation.md), [Verification](../../../domains/verification.md), [Orchestration](../../../domains/orchestration.md)

**Engineering problem:** A generated scientific model may fit observed data while remaining non-identifiable: many different equations, parameters and mechanisms can explain the same measurements. Optimizing a single fit is insufficient to discover a robust explanatory mechanism.

**Verified (source-supported):**

- The authors divide scientific modeling into **building** candidate model structures, **constraining** their parameters with observations, and **interpreting** what the resulting models imply about the underlying system. These stages form an iterative loop.
- **Simulation-based inference (SBI)** runs a forward simulator over many sampled parameter choices, then trains a neural estimator to infer parameter distributions from observations. It avoids requiring a tractable likelihood function and can amortize expensive simulation across later queries.
- For structural model search, the authors advocate retaining **multiple plausible models**, weighting them by evidence and complexity, rather than presenting one best fit. Agreement across compatible models supports robust conclusions; disagreement motivates discriminating experiments.
- The post explicitly notes that many LLM-driven model-discovery systems have primarily been evaluated on rediscovering known scientific models, with sparse evidence on previously unsolved problems. It supplies no independent new benchmark proving general scientific discovery.

**Inferred:** AI-for-science platforms should retain candidate provenance, simulation code revision, parameter priors, data splits, likelihood-free estimator calibration and failed candidates. A synthesis should report posterior uncertainty, structural identifiability and interventions that distinguish competing models. The search ledger is as scientifically important as the selected artifact.

**Alternatives:** Manual mechanistic modeling; classical parameter optimization over fixed equations; Bayesian posterior inference; symbolic regression; agent-generated model ensembles with simulation and experimental selection. Each trades interpretability, compute and hypothesis coverage.

**Unknown:** Generality to unseen science problems, reliability of LLM-proposed structural priors, calibration of posterior probabilities and costs of large simulation ensembles.

**Hypothesis / experiment:** Use a controlled simulator with known ground-truth structure, deliberately confounded training observations and a held-out intervention set. Compare best-fit single-model optimization, SBI on a fixed structure and agent-generated model ensembles. Measure held-out predictive log score, posterior coverage, recovery of identifiable mechanisms, experiment-selection information gain, simulation count, wall time and expert evaluation. Test whether agents correctly report uncertainty when two models remain observationally equivalent.

## Synthesis

**Verified (publisher-reported):** Claude Code's review practice emphasizes multiple evidence gates, risk-dependent human approval and bounded review rounds. Dynamic workflows provide model-selected task orchestration and context isolation. The scientific modeling perspective advocates maintaining uncertainty across candidate models rather than presenting one successful fit.

**Inferred:** These are variations of one systems problem: a generator can produce many plausible artifacts, but an independent *evidence and authority layer* must decide which artifacts are trustworthy enough to act upon. For code, this means SHA-bound tests, review findings and rollout; for workflows, typed plans and capability checks; for science, calibrated distributions, counterexamples and intervention experiments.

**Hypothesis:** A shared evidence contract (input revision, generation provenance, independent verification, confidence/limitations, authorized action) reduces silent failures in both software-agent and scientific-agent pipelines.

**Unknown:** The extent to which Anthropic shares internal implementation components across these products and research directions; these sources do not establish such a common platform.
